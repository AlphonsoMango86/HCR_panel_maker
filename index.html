HCR Panel Planner

Lay out a hybridization chain reaction panel: how many rounds it takes, and which of your 488/546/594/647/750 nm dyes goes on which transcript. Abundance auto-fills from real per-cell transcript counts in the ABC Atlas MERFISH dataset — the 90th percentile among cells that express each gene, ranked relative to the rest of your list, strongest dye to the scarcest transcript. Counts can be read from the whole brain, from any of twelve brain divisions, or from one of 127 structures within them.

Acquisition settings

594 is off by default — it's a real 5th channel per round, but slows acquisition, so it's opt-in here. GFP or RFP blocks 488 and 546 in round 1 only; both return from round 2 on, once the endogenous signal is photobleached.

Probes & hairpins in stock

List the probes you already have (gene + the initiator on it, X1–X10) and the hairpin sets you stock (initiator + fluorophore — X2 AF546 and X2 AF647 are two different hairpin sets). Nothing here changes the plan until you tick Use probe/hairpin inventory in the probe list below. With it on, the planner builds the panel around what you already have — even where that isn’t the dye it would otherwise have chosen — fits the remaining transcripts into the gaps, and lists everything still missing under Shopping list. One initiator and one dye per round; initiators are reused freely across rounds, once the previous round is stripped.

Round plan

Round 1 runs on the freshest tissue; each strip-and-reprobe cycle after that risks more RNA loss. So targets are sequenced weakest-abundance-first — your most robust transcripts sit in the later rounds, where they can afford it.

Conflicts & notes

    How channels get picked: dye strength (how well a channel pulls a real signal out of background) runs 647 > 546 > 750 > 594 > 488. Within each round the most abundant transcript gets handed the weakest channel (488) and the scarcest transcript gets the strongest (647); 594 is only brought in when a round needs a fifth channel. DAPI or NeuroTrace blue rides along every round as a fixed registration reference, separate from the five gene channels. Set "Dye" to a specific value on any row to pin it manually — everything else re-balances around your pin. Rounds: filled weakest-abundance-first, so round 1 carries your scarcest transcripts on the freshest tissue and your most robust ones land in the last round, where they can better tolerate RNA loss from repeated strip-and-reprobe cycles. Turning on GFP or RFP above removes 488 and 546 from round 1 only (both channels return from round 2 on, once the endogenous signal is photobleached) — so with either ticked, round 1 tops out at 2 targets (3 if 594 is also on). Abundance: pulled directly from the ABC Atlas MERFISH matrix (C57BL6J-638850-raw.h5ad, 4,334,174 cells) for the 500 genes actually measured on the MERSCOPE — the 90th percentile of transcript count among cells positive for that gene. The atlas viewer's other ~7,900 "genes" are computationally imputed rather than measured, so they have no real per-cell counts to read; for those, this number is estimated from Allen ISH whole-brain expression energy via a fit against the 28 genes that have both real MERFISH data and an ISH value (log–log fit, Pearson r=0.83, n=28) — marked with * and a dashed badge so it's never confused with a real measurement. Either way the raw value is rescaled 1–10 relative to the rest of your current list, log-scaled since transcript counts span orders of magnitude. Regions: the same statistics are held separately for each Allen CCF grey-matter division, and for whichever structures within them the data file carries — the picker nests structures under their parent division. They are built by joining every cell to its parcellation_index through the atlas's reconstructed coordinates; picking one re-reads all the counts from that region alone. The two diagrams beside the picker are traced from the Allen CCF annotation volume at 50 µm via BrainGlobe, which is a filled anatomical parcellation rather than a point cloud, so the boundaries are the real ones at uniform resolution in every direction. The sagittal is a parasagittal plane 2 mm off the midline; the coronal is the single plane in which the chosen division is largest, marked on the sagittal by the dashed line. Every structure in the atlas is resolved to its top-level division through the atlas’s own hierarchy, and a structure lights up its parent division, since the diagrams carry the twelve. The counts are packed as parallel arrays against a shared gene list and unpacked only for the region you look at, so adding another hundred regions costs a few hundred KB rather than megabytes. The imputed genes are the exception — the ISH energy behind them is whole-brain only, so their estimate does not change with the region, and the badge says so. Background: the panel carries 50 blank control barcodes alongside its 500 genes. Their 90th percentile is the noise floor for a region, and a gene that fails to clear it there is effectively unreadable however bright a dye it gets — worth knowing before you order a probe, since the floor is regional too (7 genes fall at or below it across the whole brain, but 46 in the isocortex and 76 in the cerebellum).

    Probes & hairpins: an HCR hairpin set is an initiator (X1–X10) carrying one fluorophore, so X2 AF546 and X2 AF647 are two separate things to own, and a probe on X2 can only be developed in a colour you hold an X2 hairpin for. Tick Use probe/hairpin inventory and each round is solved in two steps. First the dyes, which are the scarce resource: every gene is costed against each channel, and using something already on your shelf is weighted far above getting the ideal abundance-to-brightness fit, so your stock wins even where it is not the arrangement the planner would otherwise pick. Then the initiators, which are plentiful: a gene whose probe you hold takes one of its own, and a gene you have no probe for takes a free one and goes on the shopping list. Two genes whose only probes share an initiator cannot be developed together, so auto-arrange puts them in different rounds rather than having you buy a duplicate probe. Shopping list then names every probe and hairpin set the plan needs that you do not already have, deduplicated, and copies out as a tab-separated table. Untick the box and the inventory is ignored entirely — the planner reverts to choosing freely on abundance alone. Both export buttons write plain CSV that opens in Excel, so a stock list or a whole panel can be edited there or typed up from scratch and loaded back; the older .json exports still load too.
