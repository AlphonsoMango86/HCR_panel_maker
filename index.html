Acquisition settings

594 is off by default — it's a real 5th channel per round, but slows acquisition, so it's opt-in here. GFP or RFP blocks 488 and 546 in round 1 only; both return from round 2 on, once the endogenous signal is photobleached.

Probes & hairpins in stock

List the probes you already have (gene + the initiator on it, X1–X10) and the hairpin sets you stock (initiator + fluorophore — X2 AF546 and X2 AF647 are two different hairpin sets). Once any hairpins are listed, each gene can only take a dye that exists as a hairpin for its initiator, the same initiator or dye is never used twice in one round, and initiators are reused freely across rounds (after stripping). Round plan

Round 1 runs on the freshest tissue; each strip-and-reprobe cycle after that risks more RNA loss. So targets are sequenced weakest-abundance-first — your most robust transcripts sit in the later rounds, where they can afford it.

Conflicts & notes

    How channels get picked: dye strength (how well a channel pulls a real signal out of background) runs 647 > 546 > 750 > 594 > 488. Within each round the most abundant transcript gets handed the weakest channel (488) and the scarcest transcript gets the strongest (647); 594 is only brought in when a round needs a fifth channel. DAPI or NeuroTrace blue rides along every round as a fixed registration reference, separate from the five gene channels. Set "Dye" to a specific value on any row to pin it manually — everything else re-balances around your pin. Rounds: filled weakest-abundance-first, so round 1 carries your scarcest transcripts on the freshest tissue and your most robust ones land in the last round, where they can better tolerate RNA loss from repeated strip-and-reprobe cycles. Turning on GFP or RFP above removes 488 and 546 from round 1 only (both channels return from round 2 on, once the endogenous signal is photobleached) — so with either ticked, round 1 tops out at 2 targets (3 if 594 is also on). Abundance: pulled directly from the ABC Atlas MERFISH matrix (C57BL6J-638850-raw.h5ad, 4.33M cells) for the 550 genes actually measured on the MERSCOPE — the 90th percentile of transcript count among cells positive for that gene. The atlas viewer's other ~7,900 "genes" are computationally imputed rather than measured, so they have no real per-cell counts to read; for those, this number is estimated from Allen ISH whole-brain expression energy via a fit against the 28 genes that have both real MERFISH data and an ISH value (log–log fit, Pearson r=0.83, n=28) — marked with * and a dashed badge so it's never confused with a real measurement. Either way the raw value is rescaled 1–10 relative to the rest of your current list, log-scaled since transcript counts span orders of magnitude.

    Probes & hairpins: an HCR hairpin set is an initiator (X1–X10) carrying one fluorophore, so a probe on X2 can only be developed in the dyes you stock as X2 hairpins. When you list hairpins, the planner solves each round as a matching problem: every gene is paired with one (initiator, dye) hairpin set you own, no initiator and no dye is used twice in the round, and among valid pairings it stays as close as possible to the normal rule (most abundant transcript → weakest dye, scarcest → strongest). Auto-arrange fills each round weakest-first, skipping past any gene that can’t share the round and retrying it in the next. A gene added straight to the probe list rather than through your stock is still planned, on whichever free hairpin set suits it, and flagged “probe needed” so you know which initiator to order. Send to planning stage rebuilds the probe list from your stock, so the genes planned are exactly the ones you hold probes for; a gene already in the list keeps its cell class, pinned dye and any manual abundance, and Undo restores the previous list. Both export buttons write plain CSV that opens in Excel, so a stock list or a whole panel can be edited there or typed up from scratch and loaded back; the older .json exports still load too. With no hairpins listed, none of this applies and the planner works exactly as before.
